{\LARGE\sffamily\color{ink}Why Install a Polarimeter Downstream of SRC?}\par\vspace{4pt}
{\large\sffamily\color{ink}Complementary roles of the former BigDpol/T11 location and F13}\par\vspace{10pt}
DPOLAR Project\quad---\quad Sources checked: 8 September 2026
\end{center}
\vspace{5pt}
\noindent
A polarimeter at the former BigDpol/T11 location provides an upstream polarization reference for beam tuning. Together with a second polarimeter at F13, it helps localize polarization changes and adds independent information for density-matrix reconstruction. The second station is on the \textbf{incident beam line downstream of BigRIPS, upstream of the SAMURAI reaction target}:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SRC}\to DMT1\to\boxed{\mathrm{BigDpol/T11}}
&\to DMT2,DMT3\to F0\\[-1pt]
&\to\mathrm{BigRIPS}\ (F0\text{--}F7)\to\boxed{F13}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
BigDpol lies downstream of DMT1 and upstream of DMT2. The SAMURAI analyzing magnet is downstream of the reaction target and is excluded from the incident-beam spin transport considered here~\cite{local,geometry}.

\section{Higher upstream rates can shorten tuning feedback}

The primary-beam transport section between SRC and the BigRIPS entrance offers the potential to tune polarization at higher beam rates. The official SRC table lists an expected planning current of $30\,\mathrm{pnA}$ for polarized deuterons at $250\,\mathrm{MeV/u}$, with a source-development qualification. The published total-rate limit at F3 is $10^7\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$~\cite{current,limit}. These describe different constraints: accelerator delivery and downstream total rate. The planning current is not a guarantee for the present $190\,\mathrm{MeV/u}$ operation.

With fixed target thickness, acceptance, analyzing powers and live-time fraction,
\begin{equation}
 N\propto I_{\mathrm b}t,\qquad
 \sigma(P)\propto N^{-1/2},\qquad
 t_{\sigma(P)=\mathrm{const}}\propto I_{\mathrm b}^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Within the detector's linear counting range, a higher effective beam rate therefore gives faster feedback on PIS/RF modes, Wien-filter orientation and accelerator extraction conditions, without requiring the entire downstream beam line to be tuned first.

This benefit applies to a \textbf{facility-approved local tuning mode}, with beam termination at an approved location or attenuation through an accepted scheme. The 2025 BIS report describes primary-beam permission based on attenuators upstream of the accelerator complex~\cite{bis}. A high T11 rate concurrent with a low F13 rate is consequently not established by the published information alone.

\section{Two stations distinguish upstream and downstream changes}

T11 samples the polarization delivered by the source, accelerators and upstream transport; F13 samples the beam arriving at the experiment. For the same source mode and comparable beam conditions, compare F13 with the prediction obtained by transporting the T11 measurement.

\section{The intervening bends provide complementary spin projections}

\subsection{A 160-degree net bend gives 27.513-degree relative spin rotation}

At each station, take $z$ along the local beam, $y$ vertical, and $x$ completing a right-handed frame. In the convention of the density-matrix note, DMT2 and DMT3 each bend by $-50^\circ$. BigRIPS D1--D6 contribute $(-30,-30,-30,+30,+30,-30)^\circ$, with a net bend of $-60^\circ$. The incident central trajectory from F7 to the pre-target F13 station adds no net bend~\cite{local,geometry}. Thus
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_{\mathrm{T11}\to F13}=(-50^\circ-50^\circ)-60^\circ+0^\circ=-160^\circ.
\end{equation}
The stated $160^\circ$ is the magnitude of the \textbf{signed net bend}, not the sum of absolute magnet angles.

For a horizontal central trajectory dominated by transverse dipole fields, with approximately constant energy, the Thomas--BMT equation gives~\cite{bmt,constants}
\begin{align}
 \psi_{\mathrm{lab}}&=(1+G_d\gamma)\Theta,&
 \delta&=\psi_{\mathrm{lab}}-\Theta=G_d\gamma\Theta,\\
 G_d&=-0.14298726968,&
 \gamma&=1+\frac{2T_u}{m_dc^2},\quad m_dc^2=1875.612945\,\mathrm{MeV}.
\end{align}
Here $T_u$ is kinetic energy per nucleon. At $T_u=190\,\mathrm{MeV/u}$, the total deuteron kinetic energy is $380\,\mathrm{MeV}$ and $\gamma=1.20260044$, giving
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\psi_{\mathrm{lab}}=-132.487^\circ,\qquad\delta=+27.513^\circ.}
\end{equation}
The two-station analysis uses $\delta$, relative to the local beam frames. This is a central-trajectory calculation, not an energy-independent constant; changes in energy or significant energy loss require recalculation.

\subsection{Vector orientation and tensor principal axes share one rotation}

With $c=\cos\delta$ and $s=\sin\delta$, the vector and symmetric traceless tensor transform as
\begin{equation}
 R_y(\delta)=\begin{pmatrix}c&0&s\\0&1&0\\-s&0&c\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \pvec_{13}=R_y\pvec_{\mathrm{T11}},\qquad
 \Pt_{13}=R_y\Pt_{\mathrm{T11}}R_y^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
A tensor eigenaxis $\hat{\bm n}$ becomes $R_y\hat{\bm n}$. Vector orientation and tensor principal axes therefore do \textbf{not} have different physical precession angles. If initially longitudinal, either axis becomes
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\bm n}_{13}=(\sin\delta,0,\cos\delta)=(0.461951,0,0.886906),
\end{equation}
at $27.513^\circ$ to the local beam. A vector has a signed direction; a tensor axis identifies $\hat{\bm n}$ and $-\hat{\bm n}$ as the same axis.

\begingroup\small\noindent
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.29\linewidth}p{0.24\linewidth}Y@{}}
\toprule
Quantity & At 190 MeV/u & Interpretation \\
\midrule
Initially longitudinal vector & $+27.513^\circ$ & Rotation about vertical $y$.\\
Initially longitudinal tensor axis & $+27.513^\circ$ (modulo $180^\circ$) & The same spatial rotation; the axis has no arrow.\\
Tensor double-angle terms & $2\delta=55.026^\circ$ & Arguments of $\sin2\delta$ and $\cos2\delta$, not an axis rotation.\\
Initially vertical vector or uniaxial tensor & $0^\circ$ axis displacement & Parallel to the rotation axis; unchanged.\\

For example, $p'_{xz}=\tfrac12\sin2\delta\,(p_{zz}-p_{xx})+\cos2\delta\,p_{xz}$.
The double angle belongs to the rank-two component transformation. For an arbitrarily inclined axis, use $R_y\hat{\bm n}$; its angular displacement is not always $27.513^\circ$.

\subsection{Known transport exposes components invisible at one station}

A normalized spin-1 density matrix has eight independent real parameters: three vector and five tensor components. In the project's calibrated, multi-angle L/R/U/D geometry, one station can identify six combinations. Longitudinal $p_z$ does not enter the scattering response, while the $\sin2\phi$ term multiplying $p_{xy}$ vanishes at the four cardinal azimuths~\cite{local}.

The known rotation between stations gives
\begin{align}
 p'_x&=c\,p_x+s\,p_z,\\
 p'_{yz}&=-s\,p_{xy}+c\,p_{yz}.
\end{align}
At $190\,\mathrm{MeV/u}$, $s=0.461951\ne0$. The two blind components enter observable downstream components with coefficients $+0.461951$ and $-0.461951$, respectively, supplying complementary projections.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
Calibrated multi-angle response & Vector + tensor & Tensor only \\
\midrule
One station, or two without relative spin rotation & $6/8$ & $4/5$ \\
T11 and F13 with known $R_y(27.513^\circ)$ & $8/8$ & $5/5$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These ranks require non-degenerate analyzing-power combinations across polar angles, constrained normalization and efficiencies, and a known transport model. They establish identifiability, not a precision guarantee at arbitrary counting rates. One station can measure the amplitude of a tensor polarized along a known vertical axis; reconstructing the general density matrix requires removing the blind directions.

For the \textbf{two polarimeters with the present identical azimuthal layout}, a non-degenerate relative spin rotation is needed to add the missing projections, and the existing bends provide it. The general requirement is independent measurement projections: another controlled spin rotation or suitable measurement geometry could also supply them. An orbital bend is not a universal prerequisite for density-matrix measurements.
