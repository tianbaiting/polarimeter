\section{Executive decision}

\begin{decisionbox}{Authoritative two-instrument architecture}
  Two compact in-vacuum polarimeters are planned for the polarized-deuteron experiment.
  \textbf{CompactInVacuum-afterSRC} will be installed downstream of SRC, and
  \textbf{CompactInVacuum-preSAMURAI} will be installed before the SAMURAI terminal.
  Both instruments use a common CompactInVacuum detector-platform concept, while their
  chamber geometry, beamline interfaces, target integration, supports, and maintenance
  access remain site-specific. The previously studied afterSRC external-detector
  configuration is retained as a legacy/reference alternative but is not the present
  baseline. The selected afterSRC screening chamber is square, not cylindrical.
\end{decisionbox}

\begin{decisionbox}{Recommended first-prototype baseline}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Use a fast blue plastic scintillator, not a high-light-yield inorganic crystal.
    \item Use the canonical \SI{\ActiveDiameterMm}{\milli\meter} diameter by
      \SI{\ActiveThicknessMm}{\milli\meter} active element as the first prototype, while
      retaining \SIrange{5}{6}{\milli\meter} coupons until production thickness is frozen.
    \item Use the NDL EQR15 11-6060D-S as the baseline SiPM. Keep the NDL EQR20 as a
      higher-gain backup and a Hamamatsu S13360-6025CS as an imported reference.
    \item Design and measure the total optical collection into the SiPM active area in the
      2--5\% range. Do not treat this efficiency as a vendor specification.
    \item Keep active electronics outside vacuum. Carry each fast signal through one shielded
      \SI{50}{\ohm} coaxial feedthrough, with SiPM bias supplied through an external bias tee.
  \end{itemize}
\end{decisionbox}

At the \SI{\ActiveThicknessMm}{\milli\meter} candidate's maximum calculated normal elastic
d--p deposit of \SI{\CandidateMaximumDepositMeV}{\mega\electronvolt}, the EQR15 short-pulse microcell
nonlinearity is \EqrNormalNonlinearityTwo\% at 2\% optical collection and
\EqrNormalNonlinearityFive\% at 5\%. At 5\%, the associated primary-avalanche charge is
\SI{\EqrNormalCharge}{\nano\coulomb}. The SiPM is therefore viable, but the front-end can
still clip before the microcells become unacceptable. The first prototype must provide a
timing path, a protected low-gain charge path, and a calibrated light-injection scan.
For comparison, the legacy \SI{\LegacyThicknessMm}{\milli\meter} calculation reaches
\SI{\LegacyMaximumDepositMeV}{\mega\electronvolt}, \EqrLegacyNormalNonlinearityFive\%
nonlinearity, and \SI{\EqrLegacyNormalCharge}{\nano\coulomb} at the same optical efficiency.

The calculated carbon background point is deliberately conservative: it applies the
electron-equivalent reference light yield without Birks quenching. Its EQR15 microcell
nonlinearity is \EqrCarbonNonlinearity\% at 5\% optical collection. Heavy-ion quenching
will reduce the real occupancy, but the correction must be measured rather than assumed.

Shanghai PrMat is a reasonable candidate for vacuum-chamber integration because it publicly
describes custom vacuum-component design and manufacturing capability
\cite{prmat_about,prmat_kcell}. This is candidate evidence, not prequalification. The
purchase route must require a formal RFQ, design-for-manufacture review, weld and inspection
plans, dimensional inspection, cleaning records, and helium-leak acceptance.

\begin{warningbox}{Current afterSRC access selection; beam and service interfaces remain evidence-qualified}
  The old afterSRC external-detector design used ICF114 front/rear beam interfaces and an
  ICF70 rotary mount. These are class B legacy inheritance. The current CompactInVacuum
  reference CAD also contains ICF114/ICF70 envelope assumptions; these are class C engineering
  assumptions. Neither statement is independent class A evidence for CompactInVacuum-afterSRC,
  and the CompactInVacuum-preSAMURAI interface is likewise unresolved.

  The canonical afterSRC prototype now uses a \MaintenanceAccessStandard\ top maintenance
  opening with a \SI{\MaintenanceAccessClearBoreMm}{\milli\meter} screening bore, an OFHC
  copper gasket, and no elastomer/O-ring seal. Its detached-holder passage screen has
  \SI{\MaintenanceAccessPassageMarginMm}{\milli\meter} nominal margin, but the complete
  installed-holder release, reorientation, and lift remains \MaintenanceExtractionStatus.
  The removable blank flange carries no detector, support, locating datum, protective-ground
  termination, or permanent cable restraint. Front/rear beam standards, pumping, gauge,
  isolation-valve, rotary, and signal-feedthrough selections still require approved site and
  purchased-part evidence.
\end{warningbox}

\section{Scope, reference configuration, and decision state}

instruments. It does not define one universal chamber. The existing numerical detector,

manufacturing, and China-sourcing studies remain useful common-platform work. The current
screening profiles use square chambers at both sites, while their dimensions and interfaces
remain independently controlled rather than copied from the legacy afterSRC chamber.

\subsection{Requirement separation}

\textbf{Common platform requirements.}
Scintillator and SiPM technology, the explicit compact detector-head stack, coherent
three-head sector holders, twelve signal channels, passive conductive interfaces,
electronics, calibration, and common physics/response studies.

\textbf{afterSRC site requirements.}
Actual longitudinal/transverse envelope, usable beam aperture, neighboring equipment,
flange interfaces, target integration, vacuum services, support/alignment, and
installation/maintenance access downstream of SRC.

\textbf{pre-SAMURAI site requirements.}
The same site categories, established independently for the location before the SAMURAI
terminal.

Unknown site quantities remain TBD. Closure requires an approved interface drawing,
local survey or measurement, neighboring equipment envelope, beam stay-clear, vacuum-service
ownership, support datums, and an installation/maintenance review.

\subsection{Current integration direction}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Retain the canonical channel angles, active-center radii, and
    \SI{\ActiveDiameterMm}{\milli\meter} active diameter until physics evidence supports a
    controlled geometry change.
  \item Use a square chamber with planar service walls for both current deployment profiles.
    CompactInVacuum-afterSRC uses the \SI{440}{\milli\meter} square screening profile; a
    cylindrical afterSRC candidate is not retained.
  \item Use the all-metal top ICF305 blank-flange opening for afterSRC maintenance. Keep every
    detector support and locating datum on stationary structure attached to permanent chamber
    walls; do not mount a holder or support on the removable closure.
  \item Treat the validated 12 mm holder release as only the first motion stage. Validate the
    complete translation, reorientation, and lift path separately in the installed STEP model.
  \item Validate each square chamber for external-pressure buckling and local penetration
    loads; the selected shape does not waive pressure-vessel engineering.
  \item Use removable beamline spools, knife-edge covers, lifting points, and a stated
    maximum manually handled subassembly mass.
\end{itemize}

\begin{notebox}{Provisional inputs that still require closure}
  Beam energy and d--p reaction are source-controlled. The \SIrange{5}{6}{\milli\meter}
  prototype thickness band, 2--5\% total optical collection,
  \SI{10000}{photons/MeV} electron-equivalent light yield, populated-detector bake regime,

  apertures, rotary-feedthrough interfaces, vacuum class, operating pressure, and gas-load
  budget remain site-specific and require the responsible beamline owner's evidence.
\end{notebox}

\subsection{Active-thickness recommendation}

The detector acts as a $\Delta E$ counter, not a stopping calorimeter. Accepted d--p
particles have calculated ranges up to approximately \SI{\WorstCaseNormalRangeMm}{\milli\meter};
none of the scanned \SIrange{1}{10}{\milli\meter} options stops every branch. Thickness must
therefore be chosen from timing/threshold margin, optical uniformity, saturation, mechanics,
and coincidence separation rather than from a false full-stopping criterion.

The \SI{\ActiveThicknessMm}{\milli\meter} candidate gives
\SIrange{\CandidateMinimumDepositMeV}{\CandidateMaximumDepositMeV}{\mega\electronvolt}
over the normal accepted branches and reduces the maximum deposited energy by approximately
\MaximumDepositReductionPercent\% relative to \SI{\LegacyThicknessMm}{\milli\meter}.
It is the compactness/low-saturation starting point, not a frozen optimum. Build at least
\SI{5}{\milli\meter} and \SI{6}{\milli\meter} otherwise-identical detector heads; retain
\SI{4}{\milli\meter}, \SI{8}{\milli\meter}, and \SI{10}{\milli\meter} coupons for threshold,
timing, quenching, and dynamic-range comparison. Freeze production thickness only after a
source or beam test and a version-controlled Geant4 response study.

\subsection{Light-yield quenching}

The saturation model deliberately uses the electron-equivalent light yield as an upper bound.
For heavy charged particles, Birks' law gives the first-order relation
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}L}{\mathrm{d}x}
  =
  S\,\frac{\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}x}
  {1 + k_B\,\mathrm{d}E/\mathrm{d}x}.
\end{equation}
The supplier batch and the final surface treatment can change the effective light collection,
while the Birks constant depends on material and measurement conditions. No assumed Birks
constant is applied in the saturation numbers. Carbon values are therefore intentionally
conservative for SiPM occupancy but still valuable for defining the calibration range.

\subsection{Model}

For a scintillation pulse shorter than the cell-recovery time and uniformly illuminating the
active area, the first-order occupancy model is
\begin{align}
  N_{\mathrm{seed}} &=
    E_{\mathrm{dep}}\,Y_{\gamma}\,\eta_{\mathrm{opt}}\,\mathrm{PDE}, \\
  N_{\mathrm{fired}} &=
    N_{\mathrm{cell}}
    \left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{N_{\mathrm{seed}}}{N_{\mathrm{cell}}}\right)\right], \\
  \delta_{\mathrm{sat}} &=
    1-\frac{N_{\mathrm{fired}}}{N_{\mathrm{seed}}}, \\
  Q &= e\,G\,N_{\mathrm{fired}}.
\end{align}
This follows the standard short-pulse occupancy picture
\cite{hamamatsu_occupancy}. Correlated avalanches, recovery during the pulse, nonuniform
illumination, temperature, and electronics are excluded. Those effects belong in measured
channel calibration.

\subsection{Simplified Model of the SiPM and Readout Chain}

For interpretation of bench and beam-test data, a SiPM is best treated as a parallel array
of Geiger-mode avalanche-photodiode microcells rather than as a continuously variable
photodiode. Each microcell contains an avalanche junction, an effective cell capacitance
$C_{\mathrm{cell}}$, and a series quench resistor $R_q$. The device is operated above
breakdown,
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{bias}} = V_{\mathrm{bd}} + V_{\mathrm{ov}},
  \qquad
  Q_{\mathrm{cell}} \simeq C_{\mathrm{cell}} V_{\mathrm{ov}},
  \label{eq:sipm-cell-charge}
\end{equation}
so a triggered cell releases an approximately fixed charge before the avalanche is
quenched. To first order, the integrated SiPM output is therefore proportional to the
number of cells that fire. Figure~\ref{fig:sipm-microcell-model} is an interpretation-level
equivalent circuit; package parasitics, correlated noise, and manufacturer-specific
protection structures are intentionally omitted.

After an avalanche, $R_q$ terminates the discharge and recharges the cell capacitance.
The characteristic recovery scale is
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\mathrm{rec}} \sim R_q C_{\mathrm{cell}} .
  \label{eq:sipm-recovery-time}
\end{equation}
Before recharge is complete, a second trigger produces reduced charge and generally has a
reduced detection probability. Wide optical pulses and persistent background illumination
can therefore leave a substantial fraction of the array only partially recovered, reducing
the effective dynamic range even when the event rate alone appears acceptable.

\subsection{Saturation, Nonlinearity, and the Effect of Ambient Light}

The occupancy model used above can be written in terms of the effective number of
photoelectrons $N_{\mathrm{pe}}$ as
\begin{equation}
  N_{\mathrm{fired}}
    = N_{\mathrm{cell}}\left[
      1-\exp\left(-\frac{N_{\mathrm{pe}}}{N_{\mathrm{cell}}}\right)
      \right].
  \label{eq:sipm-occupancy-npe}
\end{equation}
For $N_{\mathrm{pe}}\ll N_{\mathrm{cell}}$, the response is approximately linear,
$N_{\mathrm{fired}}\simeq N_{\mathrm{pe}}$. At higher occupancy it becomes nonlinear and
asymptotically approaches $N_{\mathrm{cell}}$. This is a finite-resource saturation, not
the voltage clipping of an ordinary amplifier. Cable dispersion, cell-recovery dynamics,
and downstream shaping may still produce a smooth pulse without an obvious flat top.
Consequently, a waveform that looks visually regular is not evidence that the SiPM remains
in its linear regime.

Ambient light adds a persistent background count rate and an average photocurrent
$I_{\mathrm{avg}}$. It reduces the number of fully recovered cells available to a physics
pulse. In addition, any series resistance or finite bias-supply output impedance produces
a bias drop,
\begin{equation}
  V_{\mathrm{SiPM}} = V_{\mathrm{supply}} - I_{\mathrm{avg}}R_{\mathrm{series}},
  \qquad
  V_{\mathrm{ov,eff}} = V_{\mathrm{SiPM}}-V_{\mathrm{bd}} .
  \label{eq:sipm-background-bias-drop}
\end{equation}
The lower effective overvoltage reduces gain and normally reduces photon-detection
efficiency as well. Poor light-tightness can thus suppress incremental pulse amplitude
through both cell occupancy and bias sag: increasing the incident light may produce only a
small additional excursion, or an amplitude that grows much more slowly than expected.

Figure~\ref{fig:sipm-readout-chain} also explains why continuous illumination can be
misleading on an oscilloscope. An AC-coupling capacitor, CR shaper, or other high-pass
stage suppresses the DC and slowly varying components caused by steady light. The chain
responds mainly when the light turns on or changes, so a step in illumination can appear as
a short transient that returns toward baseline even though the SiPM continues to draw
background current. Under poor light shielding, a stronger source may therefore yield only
a small pulse segment without the visually obvious saturation signature of a flat-topped
waveform. This observation does not exclude SiPM nonlinearity; it is consistent with
microcell occupation and bias sag acting together with the high-pass response.

\begin{notebox}{Experimental interpretation}
  SiPM response is jointly limited by the finite microcell population, cell-recovery time,
  and effective overvoltage. Saturation need not appear as waveform clipping. Ambient light
  both occupies microcells and creates an average-current bias drop, while AC coupling can
  hide the resulting steady component and leave only a brief oscilloscope transient.
  Reliable light-tightness and a bias network designed for the expected average current are
  therefore prerequisites for stable, interpretable readout.
\end{notebox}

\section{Vacuum electrical services, grounding, and passive conductive design}

The common-platform service architecture uses one shielded \SI{50}{\ohm} coaxial path per
detector. Signal and bias share the coax through an external bias tee. Analog electronics
remain outside vacuum. For twelve detectors, the baseline is twelve separately testable
coaxial feedthrough channels. The reference CAD groups them by azimuth sector: four
four-channel coax envelopes provide three used channels and one available signal position
per sector. There is no dedicated temperature sensor, temperature harness, housekeeping
channel, multipin housekeeping feedthrough, or required monitoring capacity. Using ICF70
for the signal envelopes is a class C integration assumption, not a shared site-interface
requirement. Each instrument must resolve the flange standard, port count and placement,
connector families, and exact purchased part numbers within its own service and maintenance
envelope.

Beijing Feedivac publicly lists \SI{50}{\ohm} SMA50/SMAD50 feedthrough arrangements on CF
interfaces \cite{feedivac_sma}. The RFQ must freeze exact part numbers, connector gender,
in-vacuum cable type and length, bake temperature, leak certificate, installed bandwidth,
and spare quantities. Public web data are screening evidence only.

\begin{itemize}
  \item Bond the detector carrier, sector holder, and chamber to protective earth; do not
    rely on uncertain mechanical contacts through removable joints.
  \item Keep the scintillator below its qualified service temperature during bake.
    Use a controlled chamber bake with the detector sectors removable where practical.
  \item Use low-outgassing, mechanically secured internal cabling. Avoid unvented blind holes,
    virtual leaks, loose reflector film, and unsupported connectors.
  \item Characterize SiPM temperature coefficients on the bench and define operating bias
    limits without creating an in-vacuum thermometer subsystem.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}

The current project baseline is two compact in-vacuum instruments:
CompactInVacuum-afterSRC downstream of SRC and CompactInVacuum-preSAMURAI before the
SAMURAI terminal. Their common detector platform includes the fast-plastic study, the
\SIrange{5}{6}{\milli\meter} first-prototype thickness band, the EQR15 baseline SiPM,
optical-collection and saturation work, front-end dynamic-range requirements, twelve-channel
vacuum coax architecture, compact detector heads, coherent sector holders, electronics,
calibration, and common physics/response studies.

Both current CAD profiles use square screening chambers. The afterSRC profile selects a
\SI{440}{\milli\meter} by \SI{440}{\milli\meter} square internal section and does not retain
a cylindrical chamber candidate; pre-SAMURAI selects a separately controlled
\SI{450}{\milli\meter} by \SI{450}{\milli\meter} square profile.

The chamber geometry and beamline interfaces are not common-platform constants. The
afterSRC compact and pre-SAMURAI compact sites each require their own approved interface
drawing, usable aperture, available envelope, target/feedthrough integration, services,
support/alignment, and access definition. Existing ICF114 and ICF70 values are legacy
inheritance or engineering assumptions unless independent compact-site evidence says
otherwise. No new chamber dimension or purchased component is released by this report.

The old afterSRC external-detector configuration remains intact as a legacy/fallback/reference
route. It is useful for comparison and risk reduction, but it is not one of the two current
baseline instruments. The next architecture milestone is to close Site Gates 0A and 0B while
continuing common golden-detector and golden-sector experiments.

\subsection{Limits of the current verification}

The d--p/d--C energy values use the repository's analytic two-body and LISE++ range-table
workflow. They use a continuous-slowing-down range subtraction and do not include range
straggling, wrapping/dead layers, incidence-angle path variation, Birks quenching, or detector
resolution. They are engineering screening values, not a substitute for the version-controlled
Geant4 model required before production. The SiPM model is first-order and assumes spatially uniform,
short-pulse illumination. The chamber mass is a closed-square-shell arithmetic estimate, not
an FEA model or fabrication mass. Supplier webpages support candidate selection only; signed
drawings, current datasheets, quotations, lot traceability, and certificates govern purchase.
